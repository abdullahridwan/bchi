The gap

Awareness materials rarely reach elder, limited-English immigrant households.

Stroke and dementia education in the U.S. is designed around routine contact with a doctor's office, not around the places elders actually spend their time. For Bangladeshi elders in the Bronx, that leaves two barriers stacked on top of each other: access, since many elders' main non-family gathering point is the mosque, not a doctor's office; and stigma, since cognitive decline is often treated as a private, unspoken matter rather than a medical condition with recognizable warning signs.

South Asian adults face cardiovascular and stroke risk at higher rates and younger ages than the general U.S. population, according to American Heart Association research, making early symptom recognition especially consequential for this community.

Source: American Heart Association, scientific statements on South Asian cardiovascular risk
The program

Plain-language posters, placed where elders already gather.

Each poster uses plain, direct English, large type, and simple iconography over dense paragraphs, and is designed for a bulletin board, not a pamphlet rack. We start with three topics chosen for how actionable and time-sensitive they are.

Topic 01 · CDC / ASA

Recognizing stroke (BE-FAST)

The balance-eyes-face-arm-speech-time framework, laid out so a family member can act in the first minutes, when treatment is most effective.

Topic 02 · Alzheimer's Assoc.

Early dementia signs

The Alzheimer's Association's published warning signs, distinguishing normal aging from signs worth a doctor's visit.

Topic 03 · CDC

Blood pressure & risk

CDC guidance on the link between uncontrolled blood pressure and stroke risk, and what regular monitoring looks like.

What the poster says

BE-FAST: the six signs of stroke

Our posters reproduce existing, established stroke-recognition guidance rather than original medical content. BE-FAST extends the CDC and American Stroke Association's FAST framework with two signs (Balance, Eyes) now used by many hospital systems for earlier recognition.

B

Balance

Sudden loss of balance or coordination.

E

Eyes

Sudden blurred or lost vision, in one or both eyes.

F

Face drooping

Ask them to smile. Does one side droop?

A

Arm weakness

Ask them to raise both arms. Does one drift down?

S

Speech difficulty

Ask them to repeat a phrase. Is speech slurred?

T

Time to call

If any sign is present, call 911 immediately.

If you see any of these signs, call 911 right away — note the time symptoms started.

BCHI does not create or interpret medical content. Every poster reproduces existing public guidance from sources such as the CDC, the American Stroke Association, and the Alzheimer's Association, reviewed by our team only for plain language, layout, and cultural fit — not clinical accuracy, which rests with the original source. These materials are for community awareness only and do not replace medical diagnosis or advice. Anyone concerned about their own or a family member's symptoms should consult a physician or call 911 for emergency signs.

How it happens

From design to a mosque bulletin board

01

Design in plain, direct English

Each poster is drafted around a single topic, sourced from CDC and Alzheimer's Association public guidance, and written at a reading level appropriate for a bulletin board glance, not a clinical handout.

02

Community and imam review

Before printing, a community member and the receiving mosque's leadership check the poster against its cited source for fidelity, along with tone, clarity, and cultural fit. We do not alter the underlying medical guidance.

03

Print and distribute

Posters go up in spaces elders pass through regularly: entryways, bulletin boards, and community rooms of Bronx mosques serving Bangladeshi congregants.

04

Follow up with the mosque

We check back with mosque committees on whether posters prompted questions or conversations, and use that to prioritize the next topic.
